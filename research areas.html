Plant-Pollinator Communities:

I use a systems-ecology approach using plant-pollinator interactions to evaluate conservation habitats for community resilience and rare species support. Conservation programs informed by species interactions target the structure and functions of the whole ecosystem best support the coexistance of pollinators and plants across natural and agricultural systems (Borchardt et al., 2021). I used this approach when evaluating an increasingly popular conservation practice called prairie strips, finding beneficial wild plant-bee community functions (Borchardt et al., 2023) that were not negatively affected by the presence of a honey apiary from early to mid-summer (Borchardt et al., 2025).

Pollinator Foraging Behavior and Role:

Pollinators help transfer pollen and behaviorally respond to changes in the plant community to improve their own foraging efficiency. My research focuses on the pollination efficacy, pathogen transmission, and foraging behavior in bees and wasps. While historically assumed to be inefficient pollinators, I found wasp families fill a distinct ecological niche from bees, carry similar pollen compositions on their body to bee families, and paper wasps can deposit similar amounts of pollen in a single visit to a flower as bumble bees (Borchardt et al., 2024). I also found a common bee-associated gut parasite in four Vespid wasp species and a small potential for cross-infection from strains collected from wild bumble bees and paper wasps in the alternative host (in Prep.). To understand how pollinators respond to changes in their environment, I combined an individual-based model with an greenhouse experiment and found a large memory window was a main driver of foraging behavior in a generalist bee during floral turnover (In Review).

Floral Community Composition and Traits:

Identifying important plant species and how they respond to environmental stress is crucial to combatting multiple stressors of pollinator declines. I found Asteraceae family plants increased floral visitor abundance and richness, however did not appear to impact Crithidia prevalence in bumble bees despite higher antipathogenic pollen collection (in Prep). At my current post-doctoral position, preliminary results indicate heatwaves during the middle and late bud developmental phase reduces multiple plant reproductive traits and floral resource availability in the sunflower hybrid seed production system, highlighting the vulnerability of pollinator-dependent crops to climatic events.
